Campaign 01

The โอ้โห Moment

This campaign builds on the most honest thing about Sato Sato: the product changes people's minds the moment it touches their lips. The preconception — harsh, rural, acquired taste — dissolves in one sip. That shift is the ad. It doesn't need a script.

The structure is simple. Real first reactions, nothing staged. Hold the product reveal until curiosity is at its peak. By the time the can appears, the viewer already wants to know what's in it.

Hero Line

"Sato is sato.
This is the modern version."

Format A — Natural Reaction Reel

Fast cuts. Real Thai drinkers, off-camera product, no explanation. Faces only. Subtitles carrying the moment — "เดี๋ยวนะ…" / "อันนี้คืออะไร?" / "This is sato?" — building until the serve reveal. 15–30 seconds. No voiceover needed.

Format B — The Blindfold

One participant. Blindfolded. Smell first — what do they think it is? They guess wrong. They taste it. Still unsure. Blindfold comes off. They see the can. "เดี๋ยว… สาโทจริงเหรอ?" The reveal is always real.

Campaign 02

Say It Again:
Sato Sato

In Thai, the name shifts tone between the first and second Sato — up, then down. Thais feel it immediately. Foreigners say it flat. Every time. And Thai people find this genuinely, reliably funny.

That small gap — the knowing smile, the gentle correction, the failed attempt, the try again — is already a warm cultural exchange that happens naturally whenever someone new encounters the brand. This campaign simply puts a camera on it.

By the end of a 15-second clip, the viewer has heard the brand name six or seven times. None of it feels like advertising. All of it feels like being let in on something.

Campaign 03

Moo Krata Takeover

Moo krata is social, Thai, shared, smoky, and built around repeat ordering. It is the closest thing Thailand has to a national eating ritual. And one of the clearest real-world homes for Sato Sato — cold, sparkling, made from the same rice that's going on the grill.

The drink and the meal belong together. This activation makes that case at the table where it's most obvious. No one expects a new drinks brand to stake its claim here. Bars are expected. Craft markets are expected. A moo krata restaurant takeover is not — which is exactly why it works and why the content will travel.

The Table Serve

Every table gets one cold trial serve with ice and lime, introduced with one line only: "สาโทแบบใหม่ครับ/ค่ะ เย็น ๆ ซ่าเบา ๆ ทำจากข้าวเหนียวไทย ลองชิมก่อนครับ/ค่ะ" No long explanation. Just the drink.

Table Cards

"Moo krata finally has its drink. Cold Sato. Ice. Lime. Grill something. Order another." Copy that feels written by someone who eats here regularly.

Staff — One Line Only

"It's sato — made from Thai sticky rice. Cold, sparkling, and good with food." That is the entire script.
