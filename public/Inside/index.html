01 — Why it exists

You know how every great food culture has a drink?

Mexico has tequila. Japan has sake. Korea has soju.

Thai food is everywhere. Arguably the most loved cuisine in the world. And when you sit down to eat it — really good Thai food, anywhere in the world — what are you drinking? There's no drink that travels with Thai food the way tequila travels with Mexican food. It just never happened.

Thailand was so excited to share the food with the world that the drink stayed home. Sato Sato is here now — not explaining itself, not making a case. Just showing up, cold, and being exactly what it is.

Thailand grows some of the most celebrated rice in the world. They shared it freely, exported it everywhere, and never thought to protect it. The drink is the same story.

02 — Who it's for

The Culturally Proud
Thai Urbanite

Sato Sato is built for a new kind of Thai drinker: urban, culturally fluent, globally aware, but increasingly proud of things that feel genuinely Thai.

The discovery moment

"Wait. This is actually sato?"

They are 25–35, Bangkok-based, social, culturally switched-on, and quality-conscious. They have grown up with Japanese food, Korean music, Netflix, TikTok, craft beer, global fashion, Korean skincare, boutique cafés, and travel content. That exposure has not made them less Thai. It has sharpened their sense of what makes Thailand different.

They know when Thai culture is being performed for tourists. They know when culture is used as decoration. And they know when something is actually rooted, specific, and good.

The emotional job

Something to claim.

Sato Sato's job is not just to give them something to drink. Its job is to give them something to claim: a Thai drink that feels modern, social, genuinely good, and sharp enough to show other people.

03 — Occasion architecture

Where Sato Sato wins

Sato Sato wins by owning the moments where other drinks feel slightly wrong, lazy, imported, too formal, too heavy, or just not Thai enough.

The modern Thai table drink: cold enough for the fridge, interesting enough for the first sip, Thai enough to belong with the food, and modern enough to leave Bangkok.

Primary occasion

The Thai Table Drink

The main battleground. Food in the middle, people sharing, someone over-ordering, someone arriving late, and a cold can that feels like it belongs.

  • Moo krata, som tam, Thai-Chinese, seafood, modern Thai
  • Friend's condo dinner, food pop-ups, casual restaurant tables
  • Replaces the default beer, forced wine, or imported table drink
Conversion occasion

The Discovery Sip

The moment someone thinks they know sato, tries Sato Sato, pauses, and changes their mind.

  • Craft beer festivals, pop-ups, staff tastings, venue trials
  • Built around the line: "Wait. This is actually sato?"
  • The first sip does more work than any paragraph of explanation
Visibility occasion

The Venue Fridge Find

Praew sees Sato Sato in a place she already trusts and thinks, "They have this here?" The venue gives the brand borrowed credibility.

  • Ari cafés, Charoenkrung venues, Thonglor bars, Banthat Thong food spots
  • Modern Thai restaurants, design-led venues, boutique grocers
  • Early accounts should act as cultural proof points, not just volume
Home occasion

The Condo / Pre-Dinner Can

Low-effort hosting before dinner, before a bar, before a birthday, or before everyone gives up and orders Grab.

  • No glassware requirement, no cocktail making, no category homework
  • The thing someone brings to dinner and feels quietly good about
  • Important for LINE ordering, bundles, retail and fridge-worthiness
Education occasion

The Better Pairing Moment

Thai food is spicy, sour, salty, fatty, herbal, grilled, fermented and shared. Sato Sato can explain its role without turning into wine-bore pairing language.

  • "Cold Sato. Grilled pork. Done."
  • "Som tam wanted this."
  • "For tables with chilli, smoke, lime and too many plates."
Cultural / future

The Pop-Up Table / Thai Food Abroad

Events build proof now. Export is the long-term prize: Thai food already travelled; Sato Sato follows it.

  • Food pop-ups, chef collabs, design markets, small music events
  • Modern Thai restaurant abroad, Thai grocer, Asian bottle shop, Tesco in Leeds
  • Not exotic novelty. Inevitability.
Occasions not to chase

Clubbing, shots, beach buckets, Full Moon Party energy, Khao San bar crawls, bottle-service tables, tourist drinking challenges, wellness-coded alcohol, formal wine-style rituals, sake-style ceremony, or museum-style heritage sampling.

04 — Category framing

Sato is sato

Sato Sato has a category problem. That is not a weakness. It is the opportunity. If people already knew exactly where to put it, the space would already be owned by someone else.

Sato is Thailand's own fermented sticky rice drink. Sato Sato is the modern version — cold, lightly sparkling, and made for Thai food.

Use
  • Sato
  • Modern sato
  • Thai fermented sticky rice drink
  • Brewed from Thai sticky rice
  • Made for Thai food
  • Drink cold. Drink with friends. Drink again.
Avoid
  • Rice wine / Thai rice wine / sparkling rice wine
  • Thai sake
  • Asian RTD / premium RTD
  • Heritage alcohol / ancient alcoholic beverage
  • Authentic taste of Thailand / exotic Thai drink
  • Glutinous rice drink, unless strictly technical

"Rice wine" makes people expect wine glasses, still liquid, formal wine logic and a completely different serving occasion. Sato is not wine trying to be wine. Sato is sato.

The product truth

At a craft beer festival. A Thai person who says they don't like sato. Just try it, trust me. Reluctant sniff. Eyebrows up. Small sip. Eyebrows higher. Oooohooy. The next sip finishes the cup. "This is sato?"

They buy a can. Then they bring their friend back to try it. This happened so consistently — so predictably — that it became the brand's north star. Everything Sato Sato does exists to manufacture the conditions for that moment, at scale, without anyone standing there handing out sample cups.

The name itself

In Thai, the tone shifts between the first and second Sato. Up, then down. Thais nail it immediately — it sounds like it belongs in their mouth. Foreigners say it flat and don't know what they're missing. That little gap — the knowing smile it creates — is already the brand working.

The voice

Everyday Thai people. Not authorities, not experts. The girl at the festival who said no and finished the whole cup. The group at the moo krata table. The friend who drags another friend over because they have to try this.

Warm. Specific — it names places, names the craft, never deals in vague gestures at culture. Equally comfortable in Thai and English without making a thing of either. It doesn't try to be cool. It doesn't announce itself. It's just completely at ease.

06 — Consumption ritual

How Sato Sato is served, shared and understood

The ritual should never make the product feel complicated. This is not wine, not sake, and not a ceremony. It is a cold Thai drink made for food, friends, ice, and repeat ordering.

Drink cold. Pour over ice if you can. Add lime if you have it. Eat something good. Order another.

Best serve
  • Cold Sato Sato poured over ice in a glass
  • A slice of lime when available
  • Simple rocks glass or short tumbler
  • Food on the table, people around, no ceremony
Everyday serve
  • Straight from the cold can is completely correct
  • At home, cold first; ice and lime are a bonus
  • Lime is recommended, not mandatory
  • Never make people feel like they are drinking it wrong
UseRocks glass, short tumbler, highball glass, cold can, branded tumbler later
ServeCold, over ice where possible, lime when available, short intro, food-led context
AvoidWine glass, champagne flute, sake cup, shot glass, ornate Thai tourist glassware; warm serve, over-garnish, cocktail theatre, long history lesson
Order one. Try it. Order more.

The first can creates curiosity. The first sip creates the pause. The second order means the table has accepted it.

07 — Channel fit

Where Sato Sato should show up first

The first listings teach the market what the brand is. The wrong venues make it feel like a tourist drink, novelty RTD, or local souvenir. The right venues make it feel inevitable.

Start where the drink belongs. Grow where the brand earns trust. Avoid anywhere that makes it feel like a souvenir.

Phase 1BHotels with strong local-product curationTourist trust and international discovery
Phase 2Modern Thai, Thai-Chinese, design-led cafés and casual venuesTaste credibility, lifestyle visibility and food versatility
Phase 3Independent bottle shops, craft retail, premium grocers, LINE / DTCFridge repeat and owned customer base
Phase 4Food events, pop-ups, wider casual dining, future export Thai restaurantsTrial at scale, volume and global category creation
Hotels are a trust channel, not the cultural core.

Hotels are not for Praew. They are where tourists and international guests first encounter local products in a trusted environment. A good hotel listing says: "This is a serious local product worth discovering." A bad hotel listing says: "Here is a Thai-themed novelty for guests."

Red-flag channels

Khao San tourist bars, Sukhumvit expat pubs, RCA bottle-service clubs, Full Moon-style accounts, cheap bucket venues, airport souvenir shelves, hotel Thai-night theatre, venues that cannot serve cold, and any account that introduces Sato Sato as rice wine.
